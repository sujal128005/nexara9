Overview

A small Czech car-parts maker had to choose one of three suppliers of copper rods. A 2022 paper (Rodrigues, Šírová and Mugurusi, IFAC PapersOnLine) solved it twice: AHP picked Supplier 2, ANP picked Supplier 1.

This lab rebuilds both results from the paper’s own tables to four decimals, shows that the change of winner comes from one judgment about how much quality matters against logistics, and adds five improvements, including a robustness test and a two-year cost model.

The problem

Two respected methods gave two different answers to the same buying decision. A manager needs to know why, and how sure the answer really is.

The aim

Make the decision transparent: show every number, find the tipping point and test how robust the choice is.

How it works

Step through the pipeline. Use the tabs, or the arrow keys once a tab is focused.

Step 1 of 5

Rebuild

Enter the paper’s eight comparison tables and recompute both the AHP and ANP results, matching the paper to four decimals.

Step 2 of 5

Check consistency

Compute each table’s consistency ratio. Four of the eight tables are above Saaty’s 5% limit.

Step 3 of 5

Find the flip point

Each supplier’s score is a straight line in the group weight w, so the exact crossing can be solved: Suppliers 1 and 2 swap at w = 0.442.

Step 4 of 5

Test robustness

Run 5,000 random trials that nudge every judgment and the weight, and count how often each supplier wins.

Step 5 of 5

Cost it

Model the expected two-year cost of each buying plan, including the cost of bad rods.

Methodology

  1. Reproduce before you criticise: match the published numbers exactly first.

  2. Score price, delivery time and defect rate from real numbers instead of opinions.

  3. Re-check one doubtful answer in each inconsistent table.

  4. Report robustness and cost alongside the ranking.

Results

Learn the concepts

The ideas this project is built on, in plain words.

01What is AHP?

The Analytic Hierarchy Process breaks a decision into criteria and asks experts to compare options two at a time on a 1–9 scale. The comparisons become weights and a final score for each option.

02What is a consistency ratio?

If you say A beats B and B beats C, you should not then say C beats A strongly. The consistency ratio measures how self-contradictory a table of comparisons is; above about 5–10% it should be re-checked.

03How does ANP differ?

The Analytic Network Process allows criteria to influence each other, not just sit in a hierarchy. In this case it behaved like AHP with quality weighted five times more than logistics.

04What is a Monte Carlo test?

Repeat the calculation thousands of times with small random changes to the inputs and see how often the answer changes. It tells you how much to trust the result.
