Overview

FlexDrop is an adaptive delivery coordination engine. At checkout, a student can choose “deliver 10 minutes later and get ₹8 back”. FlexDrop prices that offer from the savings it expects, then uses only as much of each customer’s approved flexibility as it needs to combine compatible Food and Instamart orders onto fewer, shorter rider routes.

Every approved window, food-safety limit and rider earnings floor is still respected.

The problem

Delivery apps dispatch most orders independently and as fast as possible, even when the customer would happily wait a few minutes. That means more trips, longer distances and higher cost per order.

The aim

Turn customer-approved flexibility into an explicit, bounded optimisation variable that lowers cost without hurting on-time delivery or food quality.

How it works

Step through the pipeline. Use the tabs, or the arrow keys once a tab is focused.

Step 1 of 6

Offer

An acceptance model predicts how likely a customer is to accept each wait-for-reward option, and only explicit consent counts.

Step 2 of 6

Price the reward

The incentive optimiser picks the reward that maximises expected savings minus what it pays out, within viability caps.

Step 3 of 6

Check compatibility

Hard rules for temperature, contamination, leaks, fragility, capacity and timing decide which orders may ride together.

Step 4 of 6

Optimise routes

Rolling-horizon column generation, exact branch-and-bound sequencing and CP-SAT set packing choose the batches and routes.

Step 5 of 6

Spend flexibility carefully

Each order’s used flexibility stays between zero and what the customer approved, and the least needed is used.

Step 6 of 6

Re-optimise

Plans adapt to prep delays, cancellations and rider drop-outs, without ever extending a window the customer did not approve.

Methodology

  1. Model the problem mathematically, then build each engine as its own tested module.

  2. Compare five dispatch policies in a discrete-event simulation of a campus and a city.

  3. Run an ablation (no flexibility) to separate the gain from flexibility from the gain from the optimiser.

  4. Repeat over five random seeds and report the spread, not just one good run.

Results

Learn the concepts

The ideas this project is built on, in plain words.

01What is the vehicle routing problem?

Choosing which vehicle serves which stops, and in what order, to minimise cost under constraints like time windows and capacity. It gets hard very quickly as orders grow.

02What is column generation?

Instead of listing every possible route (far too many), the solver starts with a few and keeps adding only the routes that could improve the answer.

03What is a rolling horizon?

Plan for the next short window with the orders you know, commit only what must leave now, and re-plan as new orders arrive.

04What is an ablation study?

Remove one part of the system and measure again. The difference shows how much that part contributes.
