Overview

Khata listens to the sales rep’s side of a call about a pay-in-3 / zero-cost EMI product. It pulls out each factual claim the rep makes (a fee, an interest rate, a document needed) and checks it against a version-controlled source of truth. If a claim is wrong, the rep sees it within about a second, with a corrected sentence they can read out.

Every call ends with a “khata”: an audit receipt of each claim, its verdict and the exact rule that decided it. Managers see every rep’s record; reps see their own. With the rep’s approval, the call outcome and the next step, such as when to call the customer back, go into the CRM.

The problem

Sales reps quote fees and rates from memory. A wrong number, or last quarter’s price, misleads the customer and creates compliance risk, and nobody notices until a complaint arrives.

The aim

Catch misquotes during the call, not after it, and leave a trustworthy record of what was said.

How it works

Step through the pipeline. Use the tabs, or the arrow keys once a tab is focused.

Step 1 of 6

Listen

The browser listens only to the rep’s microphone. The customer’s audio never reaches the system.

Step 2 of 6

Redact

Phone numbers, Aadhaar, PAN, email and the customer’s name are removed in the browser before anything is sent, while amounts like ₹199 or 14% are kept.

Step 3 of 6

Extract

A language model turns each sentence into a structured claim, for example “processing fee, 3-month plan, ₹199”. That is its only job.

Step 4 of 6

Adjudicate

Deterministic code compares the claim with the product spec and returns VERIFIED, WRONG, STALE (true under an old price list) or UNSUPPORTED, in milliseconds.

Step 5 of 6

Correct

For a WRONG claim, a correction is drafted, then re-checked by the same code. If it fails, a template is used; if that fails, only the correct figure is shown.

Step 6 of 6

Record

The khata is hash-chained and encrypted. With the rep’s approval, whitelisted CRM fields such as the objection and next step are updated.

Methodology

  1. Keep the model out of the decision: the AI parses, code decides.

  2. Treat “we could not check” as different from “nothing was wrong”, and record coverage for every call.

  3. Scope data by role in the database query itself, not in the page.

  4. Work without a model or network too, so verdicts stay correct during an outage.

Technology

App

  • Next.js + TypeScriptConsole, dashboards and API routes.
  • Web Speech APILive speech-to-text from the rep’s mic.

Trust

  • Deterministic adjudicatorArithmetic, not inference, decides verdicts.
  • AES-256-GCMEncrypts each khata at rest.
  • SHA-256 hash chainMakes later edits to records detectable.

Data

  • SQLiteCalls, khatas and role-scoped reads.

Learn the concepts

The ideas this project is built on, in plain words.

01Why not let the AI decide what is true?

Models can get numbers wrong and sound confident. Khata uses the model only to read a sentence into a structured claim; a plain program does the comparison. If the model fails, verdicts are still correct.

02What is a hash chain?

Each record stores a fingerprint (SHA-256 hash) of itself plus the previous record’s fingerprint. Changing any old record breaks every fingerprint after it, so tampering is detectable.

03Why redact in the browser?

Data that never leaves the device cannot leak in transit or in a server log. Only the redacted sentence is sent for analysis.

04What is the difference between WRONG and STALE?

A STALE claim was true under an older price list, so the rep needs the new list. A WRONG claim was never true. They need different coaching.
