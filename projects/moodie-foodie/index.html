Overview

Moodie-Foodie answers the oldest question on campus: “what should I eat?” It is a mobile-first web app for the IIITDM Kurnool community that turns your mood, diet and budget into one clear suggestion from the canteens or the day’s mess schedule.

It runs entirely in the browser, with no server, so it opens instantly on a phone.

The problem

Students waste time scrolling through several canteen menus and the mess timetable, often on a tight budget, before settling on something they did not really want.

The aim

Turn “what to eat?” into a quick, budget-aware decision that fits how you feel.

How it works

Step through the pipeline. Use the tabs, or the arrow keys once a tab is focused.

Step 1 of 5

Your mood

Pick a mood (for example “Heavy” or something light), choose Veg, Non-veg or Both, and optionally set a price range or type a dish name.

Step 2 of 5

Smart defaults

If you leave the price empty, the app sets a sensible budget for that mood (for a heavy meal, roughly ₹50 to ₹150) so you always get results.

Step 3 of 5

Filter

suggestFood() filters every canteen item and today’s mess menu by mood, diet and budget. If you typed a dish, price limits are ignored so you can find it anywhere.

Step 4 of 5

Rank by value

Substantial meals are preferred over snacks, and among them the cheapest one that matches your mood wins.

Step 5 of 5

Two picks for “Both”

Choose Both and you get the best-value vegetarian and the best-value non-vegetarian option side by side, so neither is favoured.

Methodology

  1. Collect the canteen menus and the mess schedule into one dataset (data.js).

  2. Write the recommendation as clear, explainable rules rather than a black box.

  3. Design mobile-first, because students decide on their phones.

  4. Ship as a static single-page app so it loads instantly and costs nothing to host.

Learn the concepts

The ideas this project is built on, in plain words.

01What is a rule-based recommender?

A system that suggests items using explicit rules (filter by diet, then by budget, then prefer full meals) instead of a trained model. It is fast, predictable and easy to explain, which suits a small, well-understood menu.

02Why infer a default budget?

Empty filters often return nothing useful. A sensible default per mood means the user always sees a result, without having to fill in every field.

03What does “mobile-first” mean?

You design for the smallest screen first and add layout for bigger screens later. The result works well where most people actually use it.
